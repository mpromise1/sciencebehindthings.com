Structural impact attenuation relies on classical mechanics: managing the force profile used to destroy momentum during a collision via engineered structural failure paths.

The Impulse-Momentum Theorem

An external impulse ($J$) must be applied over a discrete time frame to bring a vehicle's momentum vector ($p$) to rest:

$$J = \int_{t_i}^{t_f} F(t) \, dt = \Delta p$$

Using average impact force ($F_{avg}$) over a collapse timeframe ($\Delta t$), the calculation simplifies to $F_{avg} \Delta t = m(v_f - v_i)$. Introducing a 76.2 cm crumple zone extends $\Delta t$, reducing peak average force by over 93% compared to a rigid structure, keeping cabin acceleration survivable.
